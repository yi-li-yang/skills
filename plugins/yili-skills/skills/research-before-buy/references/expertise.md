# Becoming the expert

## Quality markers

Every category has markers that practitioners judge by and marketing hides
behind a proxy such as the brand, the price or one headline spec: fibre, weave
and construction for clothes and bedding; actives and their concentration for
skincare; joinery for furniture; steel and edge geometry for knives; panel and
chipset for electronics; for a service, how the trade vets its own. Aim for
reasoning a professional would recognise as their own, and tie each quality
claim to something found live. Your own sense of which brands are good partly
reflects how well known they are, so check it more than most of what you know.
Learn only as much as the unknowns that could flip the call need.

## Where the money goes

A price is substance, plus brand premium, plus marketing and margin. Much of
what is sold, especially from China, is one factory's output under several
labels, so find the unbranded equivalent first. Then ask what the premium buys:
consistent quality control, a real design or formulation advantage, after-sales,
resale and a signal the person truly needs are earned; a logo and an ad budget
are not. Only the quality markers tell the two apart. The same question applies
beyond goods. For a service, ask whether a known firm's work is better, or only
dearer for its name and overhead than a solo practitioner doing the same job.
For a subscription or a financial product, the premium is the fees and terms
over the holding period, against what a leaner option gives. Telling the person
in a line where their money goes is often the most useful thing the research
produces.

## The curve and the knee

Find both ends first: the cheapest thing that truly solves the problem, and the
best the category can do. The knee, for this person's use, sits just above the
last step up that still matters to them. Translate the specs that matter into
what using the thing feels like, and drop the rest.

## The product cycle

A correctly priced product can be the wrong buy for where it sits in its cycle:
a successor due soon, a standard being phased out, support ending, or the risk
of a first generation. Where the price sits is in `acquisition.md`.

## The trade's view

Search the trade, not the storefront: "how mechanics pick their own used car",
not "best used car dealer". Look for where the margin hides, what professionals
buy for themselves, the defects insiders check, the channel they use, and the
one independent check they rely on. Prefer named practitioners over listicles of
"secrets", and accept that the trade sometimes confirms the obvious choice.

## Rules of thumb

Write a few of your own only if you can say what mechanism decides quality and
price here, how the thing fails, where marketing departs from the mechanism, and
what would flip your ranking. Give each rule in one line, with the mechanism
behind it and the condition under which it fails. If you cannot, say so and give
none: a plausible guess is worse.
