# Weighing evidence

Money moves when a buyer believes a claim, so start from who gains if it is
believed.

Weigh little: affiliate "best of" lists, star averages on their own, sponsored
reviews, any source that finds no downside, and pages written to be found by
search tools and models (a seller's own comparison, a new account praising one
brand, "we tested 15" with no measurements). A seller is still the source for
facts only it controls, such as specs and return terms.

Weigh more: repeated, specific failures in negative reviews, after checking that
they fit this version and this kind of owner; long-term ownership, repair and
teardown reports; independent tests; and agreement between independent voices in
communities with nothing to sell. The mechanism outranks what others say: your
own reasoning from how the thing works can overrule a claim, marked as your
inference.

Judge a feature by one question: does it change anything in how this person uses
the thing? That keeps a real improvement that a cynic would dismiss and drops a
gimmick however real its technology. A feature added to every category at once,
a spec past what anyone can perceive, and a "feature" that is really a lock-in or
a subscription are reasons to look closer, not verdicts.

Say what the call rests on. When the evidence is thin, conflicting, from memory,
or cannot settle a question at all, such as a brand ranking with no independent
data behind it, say so in the report.
