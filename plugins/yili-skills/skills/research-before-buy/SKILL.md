# Research Before Buy

**Takes:** what the person wants to buy, which provider or plan to pick, or where and when to buy something already chosen, and whatever is known about their use, their limits and how they decide.
**Returns:** a short brief that first teaches how the trade chooses in this category, as a few rules of thumb with their reasons and the search keywords that find what passes them, then gives the call (what to buy, and where, when and on which terms) and explains it by those rules, with the tradeoff it carries; then a research report as the evidence.

A question in italics below may be one that another of your skills answers. When you reach it and it matters, check whether one does and use its answer; if none does, answer it yourself.

Open with one line, in the person's language, so they can see the skill is on and how it read the decision: `🛒 research-before-buy active · type: {type} · axis: {axis} · {N} unknowns that could flip the call`.

## The idea

Decide as the expert for this category would: a display engineer for a TV, a horticulturist for a plant pot, someone who knows how the trade vets its own for a builder. Then teach the person how the trade chooses in this category before you give the call, and explain the call by it. The person wants to understand the category, not only to receive a pick, and with the reasons in hand they can check the call, judge the next option themselves, and overrule you where their case differs. Turn the rules into search keywords too: the words the trade uses for what passes them, in each language and store the person will shop in, so they can find and check options on their own and not through a seller's words. The agent does all the research, live, because lineups, prices and terms change within months; what is saved is the person's time and attention. Stop when nothing left could change the call (the flip test). Where the call is a matter of taste or values, inform and hand it back. What follows is a way to think, not a procedure: use what the decision needs.

## Questions to work through

Roughly in this order. Research often changes an earlier answer.

1. **What kind of decision is this?** Read how much should land on the person. If they lack knowledge, research and hand back the picture; if they know enough but cannot commit, confirm and help them decide; if the stakes are low, give a safe default and stop. Read the type (object, service, experience, subscription, financial or digital), whether the choice is between kinds or between tiers of one kind, and anything that changes the play, such as a gift, used, urgent, or a sale that started the question (`references/framing.md`). Ask little and never open with a form. When the call depends on what the person values and nothing on record says it, ask *how does this person decide here?*

2. **What is it for, and what must it work with?** The named item is one answer to a job, so search the job, and keep doing nothing on the table. Learn the requirements from how they will use it, not by asking them to specify it. Before comparing quality, find what would make an option fail for this person at all: size, space, standards, or what it must fit with. An option that fits exactly beats one that nearly fits. If the person assumes something the mechanism says is wrong, say so once, with the reason, then follow their call. For clothing, shoes, bags or jewellery, ask *what is this piece made of, how well is it made, and what do they already own like it?*

3. **What does an expert in this category know?** Cover four moves, in any order: the use, the map of the field (kinds, tiers, the white-label layer), how the trade chooses, and what fails or gets regretted (`references/expertise.md`). From them, build the rules of thumb that the brief opens with. A question that a sale or a date started needs all of this too, because a good price on the wrong item saves nothing. When the category has moved or your knowledge of it is thin, ask *what does an expert in this category know now?* Settle each unknown that could flip the call from the material itself, and ask *what do the reviews, owners and tests actually say and show?* A search snippet is only a lead. When the decision is worth it, run the moves in parallel in subagents, so the raw material stays out of the conversation. Give each subagent that question as written and the unknown it is about, and tell it to check its skills. Choose a subagent that can use skills and run scripts, not one that can only fetch pages, because a video or a page that needs a browser takes more than a fetch.

4. **Which evidence do I believe?** Who gains if a claim is believed sets how much it is worth. Failure patterns, long-term owners, tests and the mechanism outrank affiliate lists and star averages (`references/evidence.md`). Say plainly what the evidence cannot settle, such as a brand ranking that no independent data supports.

5. **What should they buy?** Two or three options, each tied to a use and the tradeoff it carries; more than one item can be the answer. When an unknown splits the answer (runs hot or cold, small room or large), give each branch instead of asking first, unless a wrong guess would be costly to undo.

6. **Where, when, and for what in the end?** Read decision prices live, as close to what the person will see at checkout as you can, and say when you could not. Then the net price of each route, whether to pay now or later, and whether another place is much cheaper once the cost of getting it is counted (`references/acquisition.md`). Someone has usually done this hunt already, so read them first, weighed by who is paid. When the route depends on what the person holds, ask *how does this person decide here, and what do they already hold?*

7. **Is it finished?** Say what could still change the call, or that nothing can; ask whether merely fine would be fine; check it is comfortably affordable. When the decision is expensive or hard to undo, ask *how could this call fail?* Give anything to claim or watch after buying, with its date.

## Limits

Regulated, high-consequence decisions (mortgages, large loans, investments, health-related insurance, medical, legal) get information, not personalised advice, and a pointer to a professional. Never talk someone into spending they cannot comfortably afford, or into debt; "not now" is sometimes the honest call.

## Output

A brief the person can act on, then a research report as the evidence (`templates/deliverable.md`). The brief teaches how the trade chooses first, with its search keywords, then gives the call. Scan wide, hand back little, and scale both parts to the decision.
